# Sankofa Digital ZA Website Visual Reset Plan

## Decision

Reduce the site to a confident studio narrative: one clear promise, three proof-led service areas, three selected projects, one process, and one direct invitation to start a conversation. Use ImageGen for a small set of editorial visuals that make the work feel bespoke; keep interface screenshots, icons, diagrams, and typography code-native.

## Why the current version feels like a thesis

- The homepage asks the visitor to process manifesto copy, a compiler demo, a comparison matrix, six service cards, philosophy, process, roots, and case studies before reaching a decision.
- Several sections repeat the same idea: heritage, sovereignty, resilience, custom engineering, and African context.
- The dark neon treatment plus code terminal communicates “concept” more strongly than “trusted product studio.”
- The existing copy often explains the studio instead of showing the work.

## New page journey

1. **Hero:** “Bespoke digital systems for work that does not fit a template.” One sentence, one CTA, one strong visual.
2. **Proof bar:** Three compact signals: custom platforms, mobile-first delivery, clear ownership.
3. **Work:** Three large case-study cards with status labels and one-sentence outcomes.
4. **Offer:** Three capabilities: discover the system, build the product, support the handover.
5. **Process:** Four short steps with tangible outputs.
6. **Close:** “Bring us the workflow that keeps breaking.” Short enquiry CTA.

Move the long Sankofa philosophy, detailed capability matrix, compiler demo, and secondary explanations to About or Process. Remove them from the homepage unless user testing proves they help conversion.

## Visual direction

Shift from “Wikipedia in dark mode” to “editorial product engineering studio.” Keep black and warm gold as accents, but introduce warm paper, clay, muted olive, and daylight neutrals. Use generous whitespace, fewer borders, fewer gradients, and a consistent image treatment. Let each visual show a real operating context or a deliberate product artifact.

### 4. Case-study backdrop variants

Create three consistent variants from the same visual language, not three unrelated stock photos:

- education: learner and lecturer planning around a low-cost laptop and printed lesson path;
- operations: field records, asset tags, phone, and route planning on a practical work surface;
- commerce: mobile ordering, inventory notes, packaging materials, and a small business counter.

Keep faces secondary. The product and workflow should remain the subject.

## Copy rules

- Hero: maximum 12 words in the headline and 24 words in supporting copy.
- Section intros: maximum 35 words.
- Cards: one sentence plus a status label.
- No manifesto paragraph longer than 60 words on the homepage.
- Replace claims such as “absolute confidence,” “zero bloat,” and “lightning-fast” with observable evidence or remove them.
- Prefer “purpose-built for your workflow” over “sovereign custom craft” when clarity is the goal.

## Implementation order

1. Rewrite homepage structure and copy before generating final visuals.
2. Remove or relocate the hero terminal and repeated manifesto sections.
3. Generate the hero visual and one workflow visual; test the composition on mobile before generating more.
4. Replace the case-study grid with three proof-led cards using existing product screenshots plus the new editorial backdrops where useful.
5. Rework Services, Work, Process, and Contact to match the shorter vocabulary.
6. Run mobile and desktop checks at 375px, 768px, and 1440px widths.

## Guardrails

- Do not use ImageGen for logos, icons, UI screenshots, or factual diagrams that should be authored in code.
- Do not generate fake client logos, testimonials, metrics, customer counts, or identifiable private records.
- Do not imply that a pilot or internal platform is a public production product.
- Keep image file sizes appropriate for mobile and provide descriptive alternative text.
- Use one visual idea per section; if the image does not clarify the offer, remove it.

## Three-pass review

**Pass 1 — comprehension:** Can a visitor explain what Sankofa builds and who it helps after the first screen?

**Pass 2 — credibility:** Does every visual reinforce a real capability or published project boundary without inventing proof?

**Pass 3 — conversion:** Can a mobile visitor reach a relevant case study or start an enquiry in under one minute?

The redesign is successful when the website feels like a bespoke studio showing its work, not a manifesto asking the visitor to study it.
